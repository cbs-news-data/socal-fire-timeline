Here’s a timeline of how the Palisades, Eaton and Hurst fires happened…
Jan. 2-5

The National Weather Service in Los Angeles warned on Jan. 2 of potential moderate to strong Santa Ana winds and extreme fire conditions expected Tuesday through Thursday.

.By Jan. 3, a Fire Weather Watch was issued for parts of Los Angeles and Ventura counties, effective through Friday.

On Jan. 5, the agency cautioned about “widespread damaging winds and extreme fire weather conditions” through Wednesday, predicting downed trees, power outages, and rapid fire growth. A Red Flag Warning was issued later that day at 2:40 p.m.

Jan. 6

The California Governor’s Office of Emergency Services announced via X that, ahead of extreme fire weather expected Tuesday and Wednesday in Los Angeles, Ventura, and San Diego counties, it was prepositioning dozens of fire engines, dozers, helicopters, and specialized personnel to assist local firefighting efforts. The agency also shared a warning video.

Later that day, Los Angeles City Emergency Alerts posted that the LAFD warned of “extreme fire danger” with “dangerous winds” in the mountains, foothills, and valleys from Tuesday morning to Wednesday afternoon, urging residents to prepare.

Jan. 7
🔥 10:30 a.m. PST

The fire now known as the Palisades Fire broke out, according to Cal Fire. Residents in the area captured videos of large plumes of smoke.

11-12 a.m. PST

Around 11 a.m., residents of Los Angeles got an emergency alert telling people in the surrounding area to “get set for a potential evacuation.”

By noon the official evacuation order went out and the Palisades Fire had expanded to roughly 200 acres, according to the LA Fire Department.
